# Plot Discussion

## Plot Narrative Card

Complete this card before writing the final narrative:

```text
Question:
Plot type:
Population and grain:
Window and filters:
Denominator:
Baseline or comparator:
How to read:
Observed pattern:
Magnitude and support:
Interpretation:
Evidence boundary:
Operational implication:
Cross-plot relationship:
```

Use `CONFIRMS`, `QUALIFIES`, or `CONTRADICTS` for a cross-plot relationship.
Do not force a relationship when the figures answer different questions.

## Discussion Pattern

1. Introduce the analytical purpose before the image.
2. Explain the visual grammar: axes, direction, units, colors, facets, and
   denominator.
3. Point the reader to the most material contrast, inflection, cluster, or
   exception.
4. Quantify it with support and a comparison anchor.
5. Interpret the mechanism without converting association into causality.
6. Name the limit specifically.
7. Connect the finding to a rule, lifecycle behavior, candidate decision, or
   next analysis.

## Common Visual Types

### Distribution or segment mix

Explain both the target segment and where displaced volume goes. An improvement
in one tier is not sufficient if another tier collapses or becomes inflated.

### Delta-to-baseline chart

Name the baseline, sign convention, unit, and desired direction. State whether
the candidate reduces the original problem or moves it elsewhere.

### Trade-off scatter

Explain both axes and the desirable region. Identify the decision frontier and
the sacrifice associated with each candidate.

### Drift chart or heatmap

Define the subtraction order and sign. Explain whether downstream stages are
confirming upstream behavior or correcting instability.

### Funnel or lifecycle chart

State the starting population and whether percentages use the first stage or
the prior stage. Distinguish reset, persistence, re-entry, and attrition.

### Sequence, transition, or journey plot

Explain order and state. Do not discuss categories independently when the
transition itself is the signal.

### Sensitivity or candidate comparison

Keep upstream inputs fixed where possible. Identify what changed, the baseline,
the desired direction, side effects, and the operational fallback.

### Use-case comparison

Move from aggregate evidence to representative behavior. State selection rules,
cohort size, expected behavior, observed behavior, and whether the case
confirms or challenges the aggregate conclusion.

## Visible Slide Versus Speaker Notes

Visible slide content should include:

- a message title that states the principal finding;
- the plot or table;
- a concise interpretation or main takeaway;
- key metric cards when they improve orientation;
- a visible note for material limitations.

Speaker notes should include the full `[Talk track]` block from `SKILL.md` and,
for CQI decks, the separate `[Evidence]` block. The notes must guide the speaker
to the relevant visual feature instead of narrating construction mechanics.

Bad title: `Q4 Segment Distribution Plot`

Better title: `Only the phase-decay candidate restores the target risk mix`
